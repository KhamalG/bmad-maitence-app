SCALE — 1:1 HUMAN
Technique 01
Stepping into three pairs of shoes
Before designing anything, the session inhabited the people who'll actually touch the product — what they hope for, and what would make them quietly stop trusting it.
Stakeholder · 01
The Homeowner
Hopes
  • Finds contractors that fit their budget without shopping blind
  • AI pre-diagnosis saves money and time on in-person consults
  • Discovers a reliable contractor, becomes a repeat customer
  • Gets a preliminary problem guess before the contractor even arrives
Fears
  • The AI misdiagnoses the issue, eroding trust in the whole platform
  • Gap between the AI's guess-estimate and the final quote feels misleading
Stakeholder · 02
The Contractor
Hopes
  • An affordable virtual admin — streamlines intake without hiring staff
  • No more managing requests and fielding calls all day
  • AI pre-estimate lets them judge job fit before an in-person visit
Fears
  • Cold or unserious leads waste their time and sour them on the platform
Stakeholder · 03
The Admin / Dashboard User
Hopes
  • Jobs live in a queue of cards — key details at a glance, click to expand full detail, photos, video
Fears
  • Information overload on the card/detail view makes decisions harder, not easier
  • Too many steps or clicks to accept a job causes friction and headaches
Cross-cutting notes surfaced while role-playing
  • Trust has to run both directions — contractors need a way to identify reliable, serious customers too, not just the reverse
  • Lead quality reads off two signals: how much detail is provided (photos/video/description depth), and whether the stated budget is realistic for the job
  • Budget mismatch has two very different causes: the customer doesn't know market pricing (educate them) vs. they're intentionally lowballing (a bad-fit signal for the contractor)
  • Platform is multi-trade long-term, but Phase 1 deliberately narrows to a defined subset of trades
  • Pricing will be tiered: lower tiers self-manage the dashboard; higher tiers get dedicated admin staff and premium features

FULL SPECTRUM SCAN
Technique 02
Six lenses, one idea
Running the same platform concept through six deliberately different modes of thinking — facts, feelings, upside, downside, fresh ideas, and process — to surface what any single lens would miss.
White Hat
Facts & information
  • Market knowledge is still surface-level — real market research is needed
  • Tech direction set: AWS hosting, Swift (iOS) + Kotlin (Android), TypeScript backend, Python for AI, Angular website
  • Biggest unknown: how the photo/video AI analysis will actually work — must be resolved with a spike before anything else is built
Red Hat
Feelings & intuition
  • Excitement: no one else combines AI photo/video analysis with a seamless, modern contractor-customer match
  • Excitement: relieving customer stress around expensive, high-stakes home repairs
  • Excitement: letting small/solo contractors scale without hiring dedicated admin staff
  • Unease: the product ships glitchy, or the core AI doesn't hold up reliably at production scale
Yellow Hat
Benefits & best case
  • Best-case 2yr: dominant and profitable in a region — the default first-thought platform for home service needs
  • Repeat usage, not one-time — customers return for future service needs
  • Demonstrably scales small contractor businesses, giving them more visibility
  • Fills a genuine market void — proves LLM-driven analysis can meaningfully improve people's lives
Black Hat
Caution & risk
  • A competitor copies the concept once proven, eroding first-mover advantage
  • Users abandon the full intake flow and just grab a contractor's contact info directly, bypassing the platform
  • Contractors game the system — fake reviews, self-referrals, manipulated data — a legitimacy/verification system is needed
Green Hat
New ideas
  • Counter copycats through brand and marketing moat, not tech alone — creative, distinctive positioning
  • Log "reveal contact info" as a tracked event on the lead — even a direct call stays visible to the contractor, and the job can still be managed end-to-end through the platform
Blue Hat
Process control
  • Decision: the very next step after this session is to nail down system architecture and define the MVP feature set
CORE DRILL — WHY, NOT WHAT
Technique 03
Drilling to the real job
Past the feature list, what is each side actually "hiring" this platform to do for them? Business model note: homeowners use it free — the platform monetizes through contractors.
Homeowner
Depth of the job
Surface job

"Get a quote for my repair."

Real job

Relieve the anxiety of not knowing what they're looking at or who to trust — give me the confidence and insight to book with certainty.

Contractor
Depth of the job
Surface job

"Handle my intake and admin work."

Deeper job

Replace the cost of hiring dedicated intake/admin staff, at a fraction of the price — scale the business without scaling headcount.

Deepest job

Alleviate the overwhelm of running a business — AI insight should actively help drive revenue, not just cut admin cost.

Direction confirmed here: homeowners are free users. Contractors are the paying side of the marketplace.
DETONATE THE ASSUMPTIONS
Technique 04
Three premises, set off deliberately
Each scenario blows up a constraint on purpose — unlimited resources, an inverted assumption, a solved problem — to see what idea falls out.
What if — unlimited budget
A pricing engine that teaches itself

The system learns from historical job costs and materials used to auto-generate ever more accurate estimates — a self-improving pricing engine fed by each contractor's own job history.

What if — the opposite is true
Let the confident skip the diagnosis

A hybrid intake flow: a "skip the AI diagnosis" path for returning or knowledgeable users who already know what they need, alongside the full guided AI-assessment flow for new or unsure users.

What if — the trust gap vanished
The AI stops asking permission

If AI accuracy and trust were no longer in question, full smart automation unlocks — the AI could auto-select which jobs a contractor should take and auto-assign/schedule them, not just surface leads for manual review.

Final Synthesis
Two threads, one feedback loop
Thread A — Trust Gap Tension

Hybrid skip-AI-diagnosis flow

Directly mitigates the trust-gap tension: it protects new, unsure users from a bad AI guess while letting knowledgeable users bypass it entirely.

Thread B — JTBD "Relieve Overwhelm"

Card-queue, low-click dashboard

Not just UX polish — it is the literal delivery mechanism for the contractor's deepest job: alleviating the overwhelm of running the business.

Where both threads converge

The data flywheel — outcome logging

Both threads land on the same single feedback loop: logging actual job outcomes tightens estimate accuracy and improves lead-quality filtering at once. That's the argument for wiring up outcome logging early — even while the full self-improving pricing engine stays a Should for later.

The core tension this session kept circling back to
Homeowners need certainty from an AI that can't yet be fully certain — and the fix isn't a better guess, it's giving people an honest choice about how much they lean on the guess at all.
