# Adversarial Accessibility Review — DESIGN.md + EXPERIENCE.md

Reviewed: `ux-maitence-app-2026-07-14/DESIGN.md`, `ux-maitence-app-2026-07-14/EXPERIENCE.md`

Verdict: **The stated compliance floor ("WCAG 2.1 AA across both surfaces," EXPERIENCE.md §Accessibility Floor) is contradicted by the spine's own color tokens.** At least three, arguably four, load-bearing foreground/background pairs fail AA for normal text, and the button system — the single most-used interactive pattern in the whole product — is built on a coral that fails contrast against both its own text and its own background. The Accessibility Floor section itself is a features checklist, not a product-specific accessibility design; it does not touch the one scenario the PRD explicitly stakes a persona on (Maria, mid-crisis, camera + possible assistive tech) and is silent on motor-impairment reachability of the FR-13 two-tap flow, reduced motion, and focus visibility.

---

## 1. Color contrast — computed, not eyeballed

Nowhere in either file is an explicit contrast ratio stated or a verification step named ("contrast-checked against AA" appears nowhere). That's a process gap on its own — a spine that names WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor should show its work for every semantic foreground/background pair, not just assert the palette is "distinct enough." Running the actual WCAG relative-luminance formula on the stated hexes:

| Pair | Ratio (computed) | AA normal text (4.5:1) | AA large text/UI (3:1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ink-primary` #23252B on `surface-base` #FFF9F2 | ~16.7:1 | Pass | Pass |
| `ink-secondary` #6B6558 on `surface-base` #FFF9F2 | ~5.5:1 | Pass | Pass |
| `ink-primary` on `surface-sunken` #F5EFE3 | ~14.5:1 | Pass | Pass |
| `ink-secondary` on `surface-sunken` (Disclaimer Banner) | ~5.05:1 | Pass | Pass |
| **`primary-foreground` #FFFFFF on `primary` #FF6B4A (button-primary)** | **~2.82:1** | **FAIL** | **FAIL (below 3:1)** |
| **`primary` #FF6B4A text on `surface-raised`/`surface-base` (button-secondary label)** | **~2.7–2.8:1** | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |
| **`primary` #FF6B4A border on white/ivory (button-secondary outline, non-text UI)** | **~2.7–2.8:1** | n/a | **FAIL (below 3:1 non-text)** |
| **`trust-foreground` #FFFFFF on `trust` #0F9B8E (Verified Badge)** | **~3.44:1** | **FAIL** | Pass (marginal) |
| `alert-emergency-foreground` #FFFFFF on `alert-emergency` #E0483A (Emergency Badge) | ~4.07:1 | **FAIL (borderline, ~0.4 short)** | Pass |
| `border-hairline` #ECE6DA against `surface-base`/`surface-raised` | ~1.05–1.2:1 | n/a (non-text) | **FAIL (well below 3:1)** |

Findings, worst first:

- **[Critical] The entire button system fails AA.** `button-primary` (white on coral, ~2.82:1) is the CTA used for "Get Diagnosis," "Book Now," and "Accept Lead" — i.e. the single action DESIGN.md itself insists should never compete with anything else on screen ("One primary button per screen... the 'go' affordance"). It fails even the relaxed 3:1 large-text/UI floor, not just the 4.5:1 normal-text floor. `button-secondary` (coral text/border on white or ivory) fails identically in the other direction. This isn't a marginal near-miss on one pair — it's the load-bearing "yes, let's go" color across the whole product failing contrast in both directions it's used. Any component library or dev handoff building directly off these tokens ships an inaccessible primary action.
- **[High] Verified Badge fails AA (~3.44:1).** This is the trust signal the whole verification gate (FR-11) exists to communicate, on a badge that's explicitly reserved for "earned, not decorative" meaning — and its text contrast doesn't clear the bar. Badge text is typically `meta` scale (13px/500) per DESIGN.md's typography tokens, which is unambiguously normal-size text requiring 4.5:1, not the 3:1 large-text exception.
- **[High] Emergency Badge is a near-miss fail (~4.07:1ish vs 4.5:1 required).** Given this badge is the thing a stressed homeowner-facing Emergency Lead and a scanning contractor most need to read correctly and fast (FR-14), a "close but under" ratio is not acceptable without a documented tolerance or a token adjustment (darkening the red or bumping badge text to a larger/bolder weight that qualifies for the 3:1 large-text exception).
- **[Medium] `border-hairline` against `surface-base`/`surface-raised` is ~1.05–1.2:1 — essentially invisible.** DESIGN.md's own Elevation & Depth section says cards are distinguished "primarily by the surface-raised/surface-base tone shift plus a border-hairline border, not by shadow." Both of those signals independently compute to near-zero contrast (white-on-ivory tone shift is ~1.05:1; the hairline itself is ~1.19:1 against either surface). For a low-vision user, without a shadow, a Lead Card, an AI Diagnosis Card, and a Disclaimer Banner may be visually indistinguishable from the page background. The design intentionally chose "lowest contrast that still reads" (DESIGN.md line 110) — that's a legitimate aesthetic call for *sighted, non-impaired* reading, but it's in direct tension with claiming AA compliance and with WCAG 1.4.11 (non-text contrast for UI component boundaries) if the boundary is functionally load-bearing (Lead Card boundaries in a scan-fast contractor queue arguably are).
- **[Low/Note] Coral vs Emergency Red is not colorblind-verified.** DESIGN.md asserts Coral (#FF6B4A) and Emergency Red (#E0483A) are "distinct enough... that a Contractor scanning a queue never confuses 'primary action' with 'this is urgent'" — but both are reddish-orange hues sitting close together in protanopia/deuteranopia confusion space (~8% of men). No colorblind simulation or non-hue differentiator (the Emergency Badge shape, an icon, position) is cited as backup. Given the whole point of the Emergency Badge is instant recognition in a Job Queue that's explicitly optimized for zero-tap scanning, relying on the coral/red distinction alone for a meaningfully-sized fraction of contractors is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

**Recommendation:** every one of these pairs needs to be re-run through an actual contrast checker (not narrative assertion) before Finalize, and the failing ones need token changes — not copy changes. Likely fixes: darken `primary` and `trust` a few steps for their white-foreground use cases (or move to darker text on tinted-light backgrounds instead of white-on-saturated), and either add a shadow-based (not just tone-based) card elevation cue or darken `border-hairline`.

## 2. Accessibility Floor section — checklist, not product-specific design

EXPERIENCE.md §Accessibility Floor (lines 110–118) is five bullets: WCAG 2.1 AA (blanket assumption, unverified per §1 above), VoiceOver/TalkBack labeling, web keyboard operability, tap target sizing, dynamic type/zoom. This is a legitimate baseline, but it is generic — it reads like it was copied from a standard checklist, not derived from this product's two named stress scenarios. Specifically:

- **[High] No treatment of Maria's actual failure mode.** UJ-1 explicitly frames Maria as "mid-crisis" (leaking pipe), and the Guided Assessment flow requires her to capture photos/video, answer clarifying questions, and enter a budget — all while, per the Accessibility Floor's own commitment, she might be a VoiceOver user, or using iOS/Android voice control, or simply shaking/stressed. Nothing in the Accessibility Floor or the Key Flows addresses: what does VoiceOver camera capture look like under time pressure? Does the low-confidence-media re-prompt (State Patterns line 91) have an accessible fallback for someone who structurally can't easily reframe a photo (blind/low-vision user photographing a pipe with no sighted assistance)? Is there a non-visual path to describe the problem if photo capture is the primary or only route in the Guided Assessment? The spine treats "stressed but sighted, typical motor function" as the accessibility persona and never intersects "mid-crisis" with "using assistive tech," even though it explicitly created a persona where both are plausible simultaneously.
- **[High] FR-13's two-tap Accept flow is never checked against motor-control limitations.** The prompt's own framing (Devon, "checking it between jobs," "often one-handed on a phone browser") is a hint the spine partially picked up — Interaction Primitives (line 106) says the two-tap discipline is "the primary interaction constraint, not a keyboard-first posture." But "not keyboard-first" is not the same as "reachable via switch access or voice control." The Accessibility Floor states desktop keyboard operability (Tab+Enter/Space) but says nothing about how a Contractor with a motor-control limitation using a phone browser — no keyboard, no mouse — hits Accept. Switch access on iOS/Android Safari/Chrome generally rides on standard focusable, appropriately-labeled controls, but the spine never states that the mobile-web Lead Card's expand/Accept controls are actual focusable elements (`<button>`, proper tab order, visible focus ring) as opposed to div-with-onClick — which is exactly the kind of implementation detail that silently breaks switch/voice access and that a "spine" document is supposed to constrain. This is a real gap given the explicit two-tap-max, low-effort promise is *for* a persona described as time- and attention-constrained, not a promise that's automatically motor-accessible.
- **[Medium] Focus visibility is asserted, not specified.** "full keyboard operability... Tab + Enter/Space" (line 116) says operable but never says *visibly* focused — WCAG 2.4.7 (Focus Visible) is a distinct AA criterion from operability and is trivially broken by a component library default (many custom pill/card components suppress the browser default outline for aesthetic reasons, which is exactly the kind of "soft, rounded, no sharp corners" visual language this brand is chasing). Worth an explicit line.
- **[Medium] No accessible-error-state guidance for the Verification gate.** State Patterns lists "pending / needs info / rejected" for Unverified Contractor (line 96) but the Accessibility Floor doesn't say these states must be programmatically associated with the relevant form fields/status region (e.g., `aria-live`, `role=alert`) — this is the one place in the Contractor flow where a wrong or ambiguous status could hard-block someone's income (account "unusable until this clears").

## 3. Color-only meaning — unresolved ambiguity, not just a contrast bug

Independent of the contrast failures in §1, neither file **states** whether Emergency Badge, Verified Badge, and Tier Badge carry a text label / icon in addition to color, or whether color (pill fill) is the entire signal:

- DESIGN.md's Components section defines each badge purely by `background` + `foreground` + `radius` — no icon token, no confirmation that foreground text renders the word "Emergency"/"Verified"/a tier name. Given `foreground` is defined for all three (i.e., there is text-colored-on-fill), it's *likely* each badge renders a word, which would satisfy "not color alone" — but this is an inference, not a stated guarantee. A reviewer auditing this spine for a color-blind user cannot confirm compliance from the document as written; it should say explicitly, e.g., "Emergency Badge always renders the word 'Emergency' (or equivalent icon) — color is reinforcement, never the sole signal."
- Voice and Tone (line 66) gives copy for the emergency case in prose ("This one's urgent — a pipe's actively leaking") but that's homeowner-facing microcopy for the *lead submission* context, not confirmation of what text (if any) sits inside the Contractor-facing Emergency Badge pill itself in the Job Queue.
- Tier Badge is explicitly text-based ("neutral ink" on sunken fill — i.e., legible text, not just a colored dot), so that one's fine by inference. Emergency and Verified are the two that need an explicit non-color-alone statement.
- **Net:** treat this as a documentation gap that happens to sit next to a genuine contrast gap (Verified Badge's white-on-teal text, per §1, may not even be legible at the ratios computed) — even if a word is present, if it's under-contrast, "color is reinforced by text" doesn't save it.

## 4. Reduced motion / cognitive load

- **[Medium] No `prefers-reduced-motion` handling anywhere.** Interaction Primitives (line 108) bans "celebratory/gamified animations on Lead acceptance" and auto-playing video — but that's a *brand-tone* ban ("this is a professional tool, not a habit app"), not an accessibility accommodation. The product still has motion: card expand/collapse (accordion), sheet/modal entrances (Elevation & Depth: "a single soft shadow level... signaling 'this is temporarily on top'"), swipe-between-steps on mobile. None of this is gated behind a reduced-motion preference check, and nothing in the Accessibility Floor mentions vestibular-safe motion. Given one persona is explicitly mid-crisis/stressed, motion sensitivity intersecting with stress is a real, not hypothetical, combination worth a line item.
- **[Low, partial credit] Cognitive load is *implicitly* well-handled** via the Voice and Tone table (plain language over jargon — "Here's what we think is going on" vs. "AI Diagnosis Complete: Confidence 87%") and the repeated "one primary action per screen" rule in DESIGN.md's Do's/Don'ts. That's good practice and does map to WCAG's cognitive-accessibility spirit (plain language, consistent patterns, single clear action). But it's never *framed* as an accessibility requirement in the Accessibility Floor section — it lives entirely in the brand/voice sections. An accessibility audit would want "plain language, reading level, one action per screen" explicitly cross-referenced as part of the accessibility floor, not left implicit in brand guidance that could be edited later without anyone flagging the accessibility regression. Consistent navigation/identification (WCAG 3.2.3/3.2.4) across the two IA tables also isn't explicitly asserted, though the flows as described appear consistent.
