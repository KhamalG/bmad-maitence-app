# Vision-Model, Vendor-Architecture, and Cost Research for an AI Home-Services Lead-Intake Platform
### Phase 1 trades: Plumbing, Electrical, HVAC — August 2026

## TL;DR
- **Start Phase 1 with a multimodal LLM (Gemini as the primary vision + narrative engine), not Rekognition Custom Labels and not a from-scratch SageMaker model.** Custom-trained narrow detectors (Rekognition Custom Labels, YOLOv11, Detectron2) reach ~0.90–0.95 accuracy on tightly-scoped defect tasks, but Rekognition Custom Labels bills **$4.00/hour per inference unit while the endpoint is provisioned (~$2,920/month for 24/7, ~$960/month business-hours-only)** — economically punishing at low/spiky Phase-1 volumes — and AWS moved adjacent Rekognition features into maintenance mode on April 30, 2026, signaling a strategic pivot toward Bedrock/SageMaker.
- **Yes, introduce Gemini as a dedicated vendor for video.** Its native video tokenization removes the ffmpeg frame-extraction pipeline that Bedrock/Claude forces on you: Gemini tokenizes video at 258 tokens/frame default or **66 tokens/frame at "low" media resolution**, processing up to 1 hour of video at default resolution (3 hours at low) in a single 1M-token context. The confirmed real-world pattern among AI-native home-repair apps (fixRAgent, FixMynd) is Gemini-first.
- **Realistic Phase-1 cost-per-diagnosis is a few cents on a single Gemini or Claude multimodal call** (~$0.002–$0.03/photo depending on model tier and output length), versus a **$4/hour minimum plus idle endpoint cost** for Rekognition Custom Labels. A hybrid "cheap classifier routes, premium model narrates" architecture only pays for itself at tens of thousands of diagnoses/month.

## Key Findings

### 1. Vision-model accuracy for home-repair defect detection

- **Rekognition Custom Labels and a hand-built SageMaker model converge to the same accuracy (~0.92) on a narrow task — but Rekognition gets there in ~1 hour with no ML staff.** The single most directly relevant published case study is Neosperience's HVAC "clean vs. dirty" duct classifier (built for HVAC-sanitization firm Alisea). Per Neosperience's Luca Bianchi, their custom SageMaker ResNet50 (PyTorch/FastAI, transfer learning) required "a couple of hours and a hundred epochs to reach a final accuracy score of 0.90 ~ 0.92" and needed data scientists to build and tune it, while Rekognition Custom Labels "reached the same accuracy score of 0.92" via AutoML in about one hour of training with no ML expertise. **For a narrow binary/few-class HVAC task, Rekognition matches a custom model on accuracy while radically compressing time-to-market.**

- **Open-source YOLOv11 variants reach production-grade accuracy on narrow industrial defect tasks — the closest published analog to home-repair damage detection.** YOLOv11-EMD reached precision 0.942 / recall 0.868 / mAP@50 0.949 on steel-surface defects trained on ~1,000 annotated images across six defect classes (crazing, inclusions, patches, **pitted surface/corrosion pits**, rolled-in scale, scratches). WG-YOLOv11 reached mAP@50 89.2% on KolektorSDD2 and 97.8% on the MSD dataset; a power-distribution-tower model (TDD-YOLO) hit mAP@0.5 0.873 while running 28 FPS on edge hardware. Corrosion, cracking, and pitting — core plumbing/electrical/HVAC visual indicators — are directly represented in these benchmarks, and they are achieved with small (~1,000-image) specialized datasets.

- **Multimodal LLMs (Gemini, GPT) are strong "flaggers" but weaker precise detectors in zero-shot mode.** The literature is consistent that zero-shot vision-language models over-detect (high recall, low precision) relative to fine-tuned detectors:
  - On satellite detection of wastewater plants, zero-shot VLMs (Gemma 3, Pixtral 12B, Gemini 2.0 Flash) achieved recall 0.89–0.97 but precision only 0.49–0.58, versus YOLOv8's more balanced 0.586 precision.
  - On plant-disease classification, GPT-4o zero-shot maxed ~57–69% accuracy, but fine-tuning pushed models to 91–98%.
  - The Roboflow100-VL benchmark found fine-tuned specialist models (GroundingDINO, YOLO) beat generalist MLLMs (including Qwen2.5-VL 72B) on specialized detection, and that "generalist MLLMs are parameter inefficient for specialized tasks."
  - **Implication:** a raw multimodal LLM is excellent for open-ended triage and homeowner-facing narrative, but for *scored, reliable* detection of specific hazards you eventually want fine-tuned detectors or few-shot calibration.

- **Minimum data to production accuracy.** Rekognition Custom Labels technically trains from as few as 10 images (1 per label minimum) and supports up to 250 unique labels and 50 labels/bounding-boxes per image, but AWS and practitioners recommend "a few hundred images or less" per class — realistically **50–100+ images per label** with lighting/angle/background variation. For 10–30 defect types across three trades, budget roughly **50–200 quality labeled images per type (≈1,500–6,000 images total)** to reach dependable accuracy, plus iterative re-labeling as new issue types are added. The YOLO industrial benchmarks reaching >0.94 with ~1,000 images over six classes corroborate this range. One practitioner (DiUS) warns that "custom labels is not as accurate with a low number of images" and that image clarity/quality strongly affects results.

### 2. Vendor architecture patterns — competitors and adjacent industries

- **Thumbtack (2026 AI diagnostic flow): internal foundation model is NOT publicly disclosed.** The April 30, 2026 launch (nationwide rollout planned through 2026) lets homeowners describe problems via text/photo/voice, then matches them to pros; press materials describe function but name no vendor. CEO Marco Zappacosta framed it as "AI gives us an opportunity to recreate that confidence at scale." Thumbtack's *disclosed* engineering work is adjacent, not the vision flow: a fine-tuned LLM for message trust-&-safety where a CNN pre-filter handles the easy majority and the fine-tuned LLM processes "around 20% of the total messages," improving precision "by a factor of 3.7 compared to the old system" and recall "1.5 times better" (AUC rose from 0.56 with prompt engineering to 0.93 fine-tuned), deployed via LangChain across "tens of millions of messages." Their broader GenAI strategy runs on Databricks. The base model behind the fine-tuned LLM is not disclosed (a third-party "OpenAI" tag is inference, not a Thumbtack statement). Thumbtack integrates *externally* with Anthropic Claude (April 2026), OpenAI ChatGPT/Operator, and Amazon Alexa+ as distribution channels — a deliberately vendor-agnostic posture.

- **Angi "AI Helper" (June 17, 2025): internal LLM vendor is NOT publicly disclosed.** The release cites only "advanced large language models (LLMs)" and claims that when the AI model intervenes, "match accuracy improves by around 30% compared to traditional methods," with users 3× more likely to request a quote. Angi similarly integrates externally with ChatGPT (the "Angi app in ChatGPT," March 2026), Google Gemini (connected app, August 2026), and Alexa+ — again vendor-agnostic on distribution, opaque on internals.

- **Small AI-native entrants are overwhelmingly Gemini-based, and they say so.** fixRAgent (AR LOGIC LLC) states plainly "Powered by Gemini Visual Intelligence | Secured by Supabase" and runs a photo → root-cause → confidence-score flow across plumbing/electrical/HVAC/appliances. FixMynd sends photos "directly to Google's Gemini AI" on a serverless, no-backend architecture and covers 10 categories including plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and water damage/mold, each with a category-specific diagnostic prompt rather than a generic "what's wrong" query. HouseDoc/HomeFlashPro and Handy AI cite "computer vision" but do not name a vendor. **The clear indie-market default in 2025–2026 is Google Gemini multimodal.**

- **Adjacent mature field — insurance / property damage AI — relies on proprietary deep-learning vision pipelines trained on millions of images, not off-the-shelf APIs.** Tractable, the category leader, trains proprietary computer-vision/deep-learning models on "millions of pictures of damaged vehicles" (and a large property-damage database), powers GEICO's AI Review, Beesafe's minutes-long claims, and Verisk/Xactimate property estimates, returns per-region damage detection with explicit certainty scores, and routes flagged exceptions to human experts. Third-party reviews estimate enterprise pricing around $5–$25 per claim processed. **Transferable lesson:** at high volume and where accuracy/auditability matter, mature players build proprietary fine-tuned vision models with explicit confidence scoring and human-in-the-loop exception routing — not a single generic LLM call.

### 3. Cost & scale economics (verified 2026 pricing)

**Pricing inputs:**
- **Rekognition Custom Labels:** $1.00/hour training; **$4.00/hour per inference unit**, billed per minute (1-minute minimum) while the endpoint is provisioned — not per image. 24/7 operation ≈ **$2,920/month per unit**; business-hours-only ≈ **$960/month**; batching to ~2 hours/day ≈ **$240/unit/month**. Autoscaling helps but the floor is 1 unit. This uptime-based billing is the core economic problem.
- **SageMaker async inference with scale-to-zero:** pay per instance-hour only while processing (ml.g4dn.xlarge GPU ≈ $0.736/hour; ml.c5.xlarge ≈ $0.20/hour); scales to zero when the queue empties (several-minute cold start). EagleView reported 40–50% compute savings using async scale-to-zero for geospatial AI. Best for batchy/spiky workloads; carries MLOps burden and cold-start latency.
- **Bedrock / Claude:** Claude Sonnet current-gen ~$3/$15 per million input/output tokens (Sonnet 5 promotionally $2/$10 through Aug 31, 2026); Claude Haiku 4.5 ~$1/$5; Amazon Nova Lite ~$0.06/$0.24, Nova Micro ~$0.035/$0.14. Bedrock matches Anthropic's direct pricing in standard regions; batch = 50% off; prompt caching up to 90% off. **Bedrock has no native video model — you must extract frames and submit them as images.**
- **Gemini:** 2.5 Flash-Lite ~$0.10/$0.40 per M tokens; 2.5 Flash ~$0.30/$2.50 standard; 2.5 Pro ~$1.25/$10. A high-detail image ≈ a few hundred tokens; **native video = 258 tokens/frame default or 66 tokens/frame at "low" media resolution (≈300 vs. 100 tokens/second)**, up to 1 hour of video at default resolution or 3 hours at low resolution in a 1M-token context. Batch = 50% off; context caching = 90% off.
- **GPT (OpenAI):** GPT-5-class ~$1.25 input; GPT-5.4 ~$2.50/$15; mini/nano tiers $0.10–$0.20 input; a high-detail 1024×1024 image ≈ 765 tokens on GPT-4o. Batch = 50% off.

**Cost-per-diagnosis modeling (one photo + ~400-token narrative), by volume:**

| Volume/month | (a) Rekognition CL + Claude | (b) SageMaker async + Claude | (c) Gemini native (vision+narrative) | (d) Hybrid routing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | ~$10–$30+/diagnosis (idle endpoint dominates) | ~$0.05–$0.30 (cold starts, min instance) | **~$0.002–$0.03** | n/a (no benefit yet) |
| 1,000 | ~$1–$3/diagnosis | ~$0.02–$0.10 | **~$0.002–$0.03** | marginal |
| 10,000 | ~$0.10–$0.30 (if endpoint stays busy) | ~$0.005–$0.03 | ~$0.002–$0.03 | starts to help |
| 100,000 | ~$0.03–$0.05 + narrative | ~$0.002–$0.01 + narrative | ~$0.002–$0.03 | **best unit economics** |

- **(a) Rekognition Custom Labels + Bedrock/Claude:** dominated by the $4/hr endpoint; only rational at sustained, steady high volume where the endpoint is continuously busy. Uneconomical at Phase-1 volumes.
- **(b) SageMaker custom + Bedrock/Claude:** with scale-to-zero, cheaper than Rekognition for spiky traffic, but adds cold-start latency and MLOps overhead.
- **(c) Gemini native multimodal (vision + narrative in one call):** lowest Phase-1 cost, no endpoint, no idle cost, instant scale from 1 to 100,000, and native video. Simplest architecture.
- **(d) Hybrid routing (cheap classifier → premium narrator):** route the easy ~90% to Flash/Haiku/Nova and escalate hard cases to Pro/Sonnet — the same pattern Thumbtack uses for messages and Gemini-field practitioners use for video. Savings materialize only at ~10,000+/month.

**Video specifics (a real architecture decision point):** Bedrock/Claude cannot ingest video natively — you extract frames with ffmpeg, pay per frame as images, and lose temporal coherence. Gemini ingests video natively (66–258 tokens/second, tunable), handles up to 1–3 hours in a single context, and stacks batch (50% off) and context caching (90% off). For video-based diagnosis, Gemini is decisively cheaper and architecturally simpler.

## Details
The strongest quantitative anchors are: (1) the Neosperience HVAC study showing Rekognition Custom Labels matching a hand-built SageMaker ResNet50 at ~0.92 accuracy in ~1 hour with no ML staff; (2) YOLOv11 industrial benchmarks (0.94+ precision, 0.87–0.95 mAP@50) on corrosion/pitting/crack-type defects with ~1,000 images; and (3) verified 2026 list pricing across Rekognition ($4/hr/unit), SageMaker async (scale-to-zero instance-hours), Bedrock/Claude, Gemini, and GPT. Competitor *internal* architectures (Thumbtack's diagnostic model, Angi's AI Helper) are undisclosed and flagged as such; only their external AI-gateway integrations are public. The insurance/property adjacency (Tractable) demonstrates the mature end-state: proprietary fine-tuned vision + confidence scoring + human-in-the-loop.

## Recommendations

1. **Phase 1 (launch, 0–~5,000 diagnoses/month): Build on Gemini multimodal as the primary vision + narrative engine.** Use Gemini 2.5 Flash / Flash-Lite for the bulk of photo diagnoses and Gemini 2.5 Pro (or Claude Sonnet via Bedrock) for complex/high-stakes narratives. This delivers the lowest per-diagnosis cost, zero idle cost, instant scaling, and native video — matching the confirmed indie-market pattern (fixRAgent, FixMynd). **Do not stand up a Rekognition Custom Labels endpoint at Phase-1 volumes**; the $4/hr idle cost is uneconomical and the product family is signaling maintenance-mode risk. This resolves decision (a): **do not** jump to SageMaker custom training either — start with a hosted multimodal LLM.

2. **Introduce Gemini specifically for video (decision (b): yes).** Bedrock's frame-extraction-only limitation is a real constraint; Gemini's native video tokenization removes an entire preprocessing pipeline and is cheaper per minute. Adopt a deliberate two-vendor posture: Bedrock/Claude for text-heavy or AWS-native narrative work and compliance flexibility; Gemini for all video and as the primary vision engine. This mirrors how competitors themselves stay vendor-agnostic.

3. **Defer custom detectors until data and volume justify them.** Start collecting and labeling defect images on day one (target 50–200 quality images per defect type across your 10–30 types, ≈1,500–6,000 images). Introduce fine-tuned detectors — YOLOv11 on SageMaker async with scale-to-zero (preferred for portability and no maintenance-mode risk), or Rekognition Custom Labels only if you have steady high volume — once you need (a) scored, auditable per-hazard confidence, (b) lower marginal cost at scale, or (c) defensible proprietary accuracy. This is exactly how Tractable and other insurance leaders operate.

4. **Add hybrid routing when volume crosses ~10,000–20,000 diagnoses/month:** cheap-model classify/triage, premium-model narrate only on escalation.

**Cost planning for decision (c):** budget Phase-1 variable AI cost at roughly **$0.002–$0.03 per diagnosis** (photo) plus modestly more for video; at 5,000 diagnoses/month that is on the order of **$10–$150/month in model spend** — trivial compared to the ~$960–$2,920/month floor a single Rekognition endpoint would impose before serving a single incremental image. Reserve engineering budget instead for prompt design, confidence-thresholding, and the labeled-image pipeline that will feed future fine-tuned detectors.

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- Sustained volume > ~50,000 steady diagnoses/month → evaluate fine-tuned SageMaker/YOLO detectors to cut marginal LLM cost.
- A liability/audit requirement for per-hazard confidence scores → move to fine-tuned detectors sooner (Tractable pattern).
- A material Gemini video price increase or terms change → re-price against SageMaker frame-based pipelines.
- Zero-shot LLM precision proving inadequate on your specific hazards in validation → add few-shot calibration or a fine-tuned detector for those classes specifically.

## Caveats
- **Competitor internal architectures (Thumbtack's diagnostic model, Angi's AI Helper) are NOT publicly disclosed.** Both are deliberately vendor-agnostic; only their external integrations (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Alexa+) are public. Any claim about their internal foundation model would be speculation. Thumbtack's disclosed fine-tuned-LLM work is for message moderation, a different use case.
- Cost-per-diagnosis figures are modeled estimates from published 2026 list prices; actual costs depend on image resolution, output length, cache-hit rate, and batch usage. Third-party pricing blogs were cross-checked against official AWS, Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI pricing pages where possible; rapidly iterating model point-releases (Gemini 3.x, GPT-5.x, Claude 4.x/5) mean exact version numbers and rates vary by source and date.
- The Neosperience HVAC study and industrial YOLO benchmarks are the closest available analogs; **there is no published head-to-head benchmark on the exact combined 10–30-type plumbing/electrical/HVAC home-repair taxonomy**, so accuracy must be validated in-domain before relying on any scored detection.
- AWS's April 30, 2026 maintenance-mode action specifically targeted Rekognition **Streaming Events and Batch Image Content Moderation** — core Custom Labels was not named as deprecated. However, the direction of travel (AWS explicitly recommending "Amazon Bedrock with vision-capable models or custom solutions built on Amazon SageMaker" for new vision deployments) is a strategic signal that weighs against committing new workloads to the Custom Labels product family.
